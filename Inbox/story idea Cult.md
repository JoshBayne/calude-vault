There were four of them this time. Usually they just stood and stared but tonight was different. Tonight they had an anxious agitation about them. Their black hoods moving against the wind and playing in the street lamps, they almost seemed to be dancing. 

I didn't know what they did or who they were but every night, a collection would form on the street below my apartment. Sometimes a single member, once there was at least fifteen of them, but always somebody. I looked through local group meet pages, asked around the building, did what research I could, but nothing ever came up. I just accepted this band of brothers that would congregate at my building and go about my life. 

The four of them seemed to go rigid. I wasn't sure how long it had been since I'd been staring, my mind had wandered too far to care. What I did care about in that moment though, was that they were looking up. For the first time in however many nights they were looking up into my window, directly at me.

A chill ran down my spine, and I shut my curtain as I backed away into my living room. The sound of the tv playing whatever late night show was a dull hum in my ears as the panic set in. After what felt like an eternity of fear, I could see the light begin to peak its way through the small gap that remained in the window. Gradually my fear alleviated and I took a look back outside at the curb. Nothing.

Maybe they had only just looked up. Maybe it was nothing, they were as embarrassed as I was afraid. I went about my day working on my daily chores, doing my best to put it all out of mind but also too scared to leave the apartment. Eventually I had to leave, I had no food in the fridge and the hunger was building into a migraine. 

Before opening the door I took another look outside just to be safe, using my phone as a sort of mirror. Nothing. I opened my door and a letter flitted down from its place in the doorframe, made out to my full legal name, with no return address. 

Inside was a carefully folded sheet of paper, however when I unfolded it, I realized I wasn't sure what sort of material it was made of. Where creases and fold lines should be, there was nothing, it was as if the paper was brand new. Written upon it was an apology letter, explaining that there was no reason to be alarmed as to the "proceedings" last night, and that my name had been gathered from my address. 

My sense of unease stayed at a relatively constant level. They claimed to be harmless but they also knew where I lived and personal details about me.
