# Gardner's, Chapters 14–15

## Summary

Chapter 15 covers late medieval and early Renaissance northern Europe (15th century). Likely reading for ARTS 1304 (Fall 2023); the syllabus file `Fall-2023-ARTS-1304` has images only, so the link is an inference.

## Key points
- Ch. 14: Scholars disagree whether 1200–1400 Italy is late medieval or proto-Renaissance. Giotto (Padua Arena Chapel frescoes, 1305, paid for by a banker to atone for usury) turned painting toward natural appearance. Duccio of Siena kept Byzantine forms but made figures show feeling. Ambrogio Lorenzetti painted city and country scenes in Siena's town hall, among the first landscapes since antiquity. Nicola and Giovanni Pisano carved pulpits (Roman and Gothic influences). The Black Death and the papal Great Schism are context.
- Ch. 15: Dukes of Burgundy ruled Flanders and paid for art. Oil paint on wood spread (Campin, van Eyck, Rogier van der Weyden); portraits became a main product for merchants. Hidden symbols in daily objects (Mérode Altarpiece). Printing and woodcut and engraving (Schongauer) made art cheaper. German late-Gothic carved altarpieces (Stoss, Riemenschneider).
- Sidebars cover fresco, tempera and oil technique, artists' guilds and contracts, artist training, and book illustration.
