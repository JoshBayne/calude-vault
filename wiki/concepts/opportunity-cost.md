# Opportunity cost

## Definition
The value of the best alternative given up when a choice is made; the notes treat it as a ratio (what you give up ÷ what you gain) that rises as production of a good expands.

## How it works / key claims
- Choices are made at the margin: do an activity while marginal benefit exceeds marginal cost, and stop where MB = MC.
- Comparative advantage means the lower opportunity cost, which is what makes trade beneficial.
- Sunk costs are not opportunity costs: fixed costs are ignored in a short-run shutdown decision.

## Evidence and examples
- PPF, trade and the Liz/Joe smoothie-and-salad example — [[sources/uhcl-economics-notes]]
- **Transfer pricing with idle capacity** (Dart Industries exercise): a division with spare capacity has an opportunity cost equal to its $125 variable cost, so the lowest acceptable transfer price is $125 and the highest is the $180 outside price — [[sources/uhcl-accounting-notes]] (range recomputed by me).
- **Regret as opportunity loss**: decision analysis defines regret as the payoff forgone by not choosing the best alternative in a state of nature — [[sources/uhcl-quantitative-notes]]. *Inference:* this is opportunity cost applied to uncertain outcomes (see [[concepts/decision-analysis-and-regret]]).
- **Budgeting**: the 50/30/20 rule and the weekly goal checklists are allocation problems under scarce income and time — [[sources/uhcl-finance-notes]], [[sources/personal-notes]].
