# Break-even and profit functions

## Definition
Profit P(x) = R(x) − C(x); the break-even quantity is where revenue equals cost. With price P, variable cost V and fixed cost F, linear break-even is Q = F ÷ (P − V).

## How it works / key claims
- Slopes of linear cost, revenue and profit functions are marginal cost, revenue and profit; P(0) = −fixed cost.
- A competitive firm maximizes profit where MR = MC, shuts down in the short run if P < AVC (fixed costs are sunk) and exits in the long run if P < ATC.
- Overhead is allocated using a predetermined rate (est. overhead ÷ est. activity base), and the over/under-application is closed to COGS.

- Linear profit examples: receivers P(x) = 220x − 40,000 gives P(500) = 70,000; belts break even at 400 units — [[sources/uhcl-quantitative-notes]]
- "Bagels R Us": F = $100, V = $0.50, price $1 → 200 bagels — [[sources/uhcl-quantitative-notes]]
