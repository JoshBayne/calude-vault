# Externalities and public goods

## Definition
An **externality** is a cost or benefit of a market exchange borne by a third party. A **public good** is non-rival and non-excludable; a **common resource** is rival but non-excludable. Both lead to market failure.

## How it works / key claims
- Goods are classified by excludability and rivalry: private, club, common resource, public.
- Free riders lead to under-provision of public goods; the tragedy of the commons arises from a negative externality on shared resources; remedies are corrective taxes, regulation, permit auctions or privatization.
- A negative externality shifts supply relative to social cost and creates deadweight loss. Well-defined property rights (exclusivity, transferability, enforceability) are the tool for internalizing them.
- Pollutants split into fund pollutants (the environment can absorb them) and stock pollutants (they accumulate: cadmium, lead, mercury); point sources are easier to control than nonpoint sources.
