# Market power and cartels

## Definition
Market power is the ability to set price above marginal cost, arising from barriers to entry (key resources, government grant, natural monopoly) or from coordination among a few sellers.

## How it works / key claims
- A monopolist faces the whole demand curve so MR < P, produces where MR = MC, charges P > MC and creates deadweight loss. Policy options: antitrust (Sherman 1890, Clayton 1914), regulation, public ownership or inaction.
- Oligopoly outcomes are analyzed with game theory: a cartel's output is monopoly-like but each member gains by cheating, so collusion is unstable; Nash equilibrium lies between monopoly and competitive output; repeated games allow cooperation (tit-for-tat).
- **Legal monopolies**: patents (20 years), copyrights, trademarks and trade secrets give temporary exclusivity — [[sources/uhcl-environmental-economics-and-law-notes]]. *Inference:* IP law is the government-grant barrier to entry described in the economics notes, deliberately created to reward innovation at the cost of some deadweight loss.
