# Intermediate Accounting (ACCT 3341)

Summer 2023 course using **Kieso, Intermediate Accounting 18e** (Wiley) slide decks, with quizzes and exam reviews. Builds on [[topics/financial-accounting]] and [[topics/managerial-accounting]]; time-value-of-money chapters connect to [[topics/corporate-finance]]. Part of [[topics/uhcl-coursework]].

> The OneNote files `Chapter-N-PowerPoint` map to Kieso chapters: 2 Accounting Information System, 3 Income Statement and Revenue Recognition, 4 Balance Sheet and Cash Flows, 5 Time Value of Money, 6 Cash and Receivables, 7 Valuation of Inventories, 8 Inventories: Additional Valuation Issues, 9 Acquisition and Disposition of PP&E, 10 Depreciation/Impairments/Depletion, 11 Intangible Assets, 22 Statement of Cash Flows. **Most hold only a link to the original PPTX**; only Chapters 8 and 10 have extracted slide text.

## Core concepts captured

### Revenue and expense recognition (`Untitled`)
- **Revenue recognition**: record revenue when earned (service performed or goods delivered), measured by assets received (cash or receivables).
- **Expense recognition / matching**: expenses incurred to generate revenue are reported in the same period as that revenue.

### Inventory valuation (Ch. 8)
- **Net realizable value (NRV)** = estimated selling price − cost of completion − selling costs. Slide example: $1,000 − $50 − $200 = **$750**.
- **Lower of cost and NRV (LCNRV)**: usually applied item by item. Whole Foods example: items valued at the lower of cost or NRV sum to **$384,000** (recomputed: 80k + 100k + 40k + 72k + 92k).
- Write-down journal options: **loss method** (debit Inventory Loss) or **cost-of-goods-sold method** (debit COGS), both crediting Inventory; example write-down $82,000 → $70,000 = **$12,000**.
- Practice problem (Gard Corporation, four items M–P) asks for LCNRV per item and the write-down item-by-item vs on total inventory.

### Depreciation (Ch. 10)
- **Depreciation** allocates the cost of tangible assets to expense over the periods that benefit. Terminology: fixed assets → *depreciation*; intangibles → *amortization*; natural resources → *depletion*.

### Quiz 1 and Quiz 2 answer keys
- **Quiz 1**: FASB conceptual framework (the *non*-benefit is that accountants become less needed); converting accruals to cash, e.g. cash received for interest when interest receivable rose from $15,000 to $18,200 on $141,000 of revenue → **$137,800** (recomputed).
- **Quiz 2**: managing earnings down (shortening an equipment life from 10 to 8 years); prior-period correction: ending retained earnings = 6,000,000 − 1,290,000 + 3,000,000 − 960,000 = **$6,750,000**; a three-item prior-period/loss problem at a 20% tax rate.
- Quiz 3–6 answer files are empty stubs or image-only (Quiz 3 and 4 are very large image dumps).

### Review handouts
- `Ex1 Review Questions` repeats the Dolan interest-receivable problem and adds a note-payable accrued-interest question (a $1M note at 9% issued Sept 1, 2020).
- `Exam3 Review Handout` covers **lower-of-cost-or-market** with replacement cost, **gross profit method** for a fire-loss estimate (50% markup on cost) and the **retail inventory method** (cost and retail columns).
- `3rd Exam Review` (course not stated; content is introductory financial accounting, so it may belong with [[topics/financial-accounting]]): accrued revenues/expenses, unearned revenue, contingent liabilities (probable and estimable → record and disclose; warranty expense recorded at sale).
