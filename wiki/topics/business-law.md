# Business Law

Two exam-review outlines for a business-law course (course number and term not stated in the files). Ties to [[topics/information-systems-and-security]] (spam, cybersquatting) and ethics/CSR themes in [[topics/public-speaking]]. Part of [[topics/uhcl-coursework]].

## Exam 1: Legal system and constitutional law
- **Parties**: plaintiff/petitioner sues; defendant/respondent is sued; appellant appeals; appellee defends the lower decision.
- **Opinion types**: unanimous, majority, concurring, dissenting, plurality (at least five judges agree on the result but not a single rationale), per curiam (unsigned).
- **Stare decisis** ("stand on decided cases"): decide by precedent; higher-court decisions bind lower courts; overturning requires compelling reasons. **Case of first impression**: no precedent, so courts may use persuasive authority, policy or social values.
- **Sources of law**: primary (constitutions, statutes, administrative regulations, case/common law) vs secondary (books and articles).
- **Jurisdiction**: *in personam* (people) vs *in rem* (property); long-arm statutes and **minimum contacts**; general vs limited (probate, bankruptcy); original vs appellate. Federal courts have exclusive jurisdiction over federal crimes, bankruptcy, most patent/copyright cases and suits against the US; state courts over state-law matters (divorce, adoption); **concurrent** jurisdiction includes diversity cases.
- **Procedure**: default judgments, discovery (depositions are sworn testimony; interrogatories are written questions answered under oath), voir dire (jury selection), fee arrangements (fixed, hourly, contingency), post-trial motions (new trial; **JNOV**). Appeals courts hear no new evidence and rarely overturn trial results. Mandatory binding arbitration clauses are generally enforceable.
- **Constitution**: commerce clause (including the dormant commerce clause and *Gibbons v. Ogden*), federalism and the 10th Amendment, taxing and spending, privileges and immunities, Bill of Rights (applied to the states through the 14th Amendment), supremacy clause and preemption, judicial review, separation of powers.
- **Ethics**: *moral minimum* (mere legal compliance) vs ethical behavior; corporate social responsibility toward stakeholders.

## Exam 2: IP, contracts, crime and international law
- **Intellectual property**: patent (20 years; up to treble damages for intentional infringement); copyright (automatic, protects expression; author's life + 70 years, or 95 years from publication / 120 from creation for corporate works); trademark (unlimited while in use; renew between years 5–6, then every 10 years); trade secret (unlimited while secret). Permission to use IP comes through **licensing**.
- **Trademark infringement** (likelihood of confusion) vs **dilution** (famous marks; no confusion needed).
- **Spam**: 37 states require opt-out; federal **CAN-SPAM Act of 2003** bans false and deceptive email and preempts most state spam law; the **US SAFE WEB Act** lets the FTC cooperate with foreign agencies.
- **Cybersquatting** (ACPA): registering a name identical or confusingly similar to another's trademark with bad-faith intent to profit; *typosquatting* uses misspellings.
- **Crime**: felony (death or prison over one year) vs misdemeanor; needs *actus reus* and *mens rea*. Burden of proof: **preponderance** (civil) vs **beyond a reasonable doubt** (criminal).
- **Contracts**: valid contract needs agreement (offer + acceptance), consideration, capacity, legality. Offers end by revocation, rejection, counteroffer, lapse, destruction of subject matter, death/incompetence or supervening illegality. Mistakes of material fact make contracts voidable (bilateral: either party may rescind; unilateral usually not). Defenses: lack of voluntary consent (mistake, misrepresentation, undue influence, duress) or wrong form. Intended third-party beneficiaries can sue. **UCC Article 2/2A** concepts: *cover* (buyer buys substitutes and recovers the difference) and *cure* (seller corrects a nonconforming delivery within the contract period).
- **International**: extraterritorial application presumed not to apply; *dumping* and antidumping duties; tariffs vs quotas; expropriation (public purpose, just compensation) vs confiscation; **comity**, **act of state doctrine**, **sovereign immunity** and its FSIA exceptions (waiver, commercial activity with US effect, torts in the US, state sponsors of terrorism).
