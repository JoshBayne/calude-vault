# Art history: late medieval Italy and northern Europe

## Current synthesis
- **Italy, 1200s–1300s**: three styles lived side by side: Italo-Byzantine (Berlinghieri, Cimabue), French Gothic (Giovanni Pisano) and revived classical realism (Nicola Pisano, Cavallini, then Giotto). Giotto is often called the first Renaissance painter because he painted solid, feeling figures in believable space. Source: [[sources/gardners-art-history-ch14-15]].
- **Regional centers**: Florence, Siena and Pisa were trade cities; Venice and Genoa were sea powers. Town halls and cathedrals show local style (Orvieto facade mixes Gothic detail with an older timber-roofed church).
- **Northern Europe, 1400s**: Burgundian dukes and Flemish merchants paid for art. Oil paint gave rich glazes. Portrait painting grew. Symbolism sits in ordinary objects. Printing and prints widened the audience.

## Points of agreement
The book presents these as settled. The "late medieval or proto-Renaissance" label is the one open debate it names.
