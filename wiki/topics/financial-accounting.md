# Financial Accounting (ACCT 2301)

Notes from UHCL **ACCT 2301 – Principles of Accounting I**, built around a Cengage-style textbook (the recurring **NetSolutions** example). Continues in [[topics/managerial-accounting]] (ACCT 2302-level material). Debit/credit logic also underpins the AP notes in [[topics/work-and-web-projects]]. Part of [[topics/uhcl-coursework]].

## Core concepts

### Chapter 1: Business and accounting
- A **business** assembles resources to provide goods/services; **profit** = revenue − expenses. Business types: service (Delta, Disney), merchandising (Wal-Mart, Amazon), manufacturing (Ford, Merck).
- **GAAP** is set primarily by **FASB**; the **SEC** oversees public-company disclosure; the **IASB** sets international standards used by over half the world's nations.
- Required statements: balance sheet, income statement, comprehensive income, statement of changes in equity, cash flow statement. Annual report = **10-K** (20-F for foreign filers); financial statements are Item 8.
- Reading tips from class: statements show 3 years of data; watch rounding and currency; "net earnings" = net income; 52/53-week fiscal years exist.
- Key concepts: **historical cost** vs **fair value**, **accrual basis** (cash basis for tax only), **matching concept**, dividends, shareholders (corporations) vs members (LLCs).
- **Accounting equation**: Assets = Liabilities + Equity. Personal version: assets − liabilities = net equity.

### Chapter 2: Recording transactions
- **Double-entry**: every transaction hits at least two accounts and debits = credits.
- **Rules**: assets and expenses (and dividends) increase with debits; liabilities, equity and revenues increase with credits. "Normal balance" = side that receives increases; balances opposite the norm appear in brackets; contra accounts have the opposite norm.
- **Chart of accounts** is ordered as on the statements: assets, liabilities, equity, revenues, expenses. Unearned revenue is a liability (cash received before service).
- **Process**: journalize → post to ledger (T accounts) → trial balance. Four-step analysis: identify account type, increase/decrease, debit/credit, write entry.
- **NetSolutions** transactions a–h are journalized in full (stock issuance $25,000, land $20,000, supplies on account $1,350, fees $7,500, expenses $3,650, payment on account $950, supplies used $800, dividends $2,000).
- **Trial balance**: verifies equal debits/credits. Error-finding heuristics: difference divisible by 10/100/1000 → addition error; by 2 → debit/credit flipped; by 9 → **transposition or slide**. Errors that don't unbalance (wrong account) are fixed with a **correcting entry** (example: $12,500 equipment wrongly debited to Supplies).
- **Horizontal analysis**: current vs prior period amounts and percent change, illustrated with J. Holmes, Attorney-at-Law (net income +19.9%).

### Chapter 3: Adjusting entries (`3-0 Lecture Notes`, added 2026-10-30)
- GAAP uses an **accounting period** (never longer than 12 months) and the **matching principle**; revenue is recognized when **earned**; the cash basis is for tax only.
- **Adjusting entries** make statements comply with GAAP. Four types: **prepaid expenses** (asset), **accrued revenue** (asset), **unearned revenue** (liability), **accrued expenses** (liability).
- **Fixed assets** last over a year; **depreciation** expenses cost over useful life; **accumulated depreciation** is a contra asset; **book value** = cost − accumulated depreciation. **Vertical analysis** compares amounts within one year (contrast with horizontal analysis in Chapter 2).
- Rounding rule from the note: do not add zeros to numbers (use 118.861 billion or 118,861 million).

### Chapter 4 (`4-0 Lecture Notes`)
- Only three learning objectives: financial statements from adjusted balances, closing entries, the accounting cycle for one period.
