# Managerial Accounting (ACCT 2302)

Cost-accounting notes for manufacturers, following on from [[topics/financial-accounting]]. Related decision-making ideas (differential analysis, capital investment) connect to [[topics/corporate-finance]] and the marginal-analysis rule in [[topics/economics]]. Part of [[topics/uhcl-coursework]].

> Note: files named `Chapter 15 notes` and `Chapter 16-17` look like accounting chapter numbers but are actually **microeconomics**; they are summarized in [[topics/economics]].

## Core concepts

### Chapter 15: Introduction
- **Direct costs** trace to a cost object (wood for a guitar); **indirect costs** do not (production-manager salaries).
- A finished product's cost = **direct materials + direct labor + factory overhead**. **Prime cost** = DM + DL; **conversion cost** = DL + FOH.
- **Product costs** sit in inventory on the balance sheet until sold, then become COGS; **period costs** hit the income statement immediately.
- Three inventories for manufacturers: **raw materials, work in process, finished goods**. Retailers compute COGS from beginning inventory + net purchases − ending inventory; manufacturers need a **statement of cost of goods manufactured**.

### Chapter 16: Job order costing
- Used for custom or batch production (apparel, guitars). Costs flow WIP → finished goods → COGS.
- Materials are tracked in a **materials ledger** (debits from receiving reports, credits from requisitions).
- **Predetermined factory overhead rate** = estimated total overhead ÷ estimated activity base (direct labor hours, labor cost, or machine hours). Example: $50,000 ÷ 10,000 DLH = **$5 per DLH** (Legend Guitars).
- Applied vs actual overhead: applied < actual = **underapplied** (debit balance); applied > actual = **overapplied** (credit balance). The year-end balance is closed to **COGS**, not carried to the next year.

### Chapter 17: Process cost systems
- Start by determining the units to be assigned costs; compute **equivalent units** separately for materials and conversion costs (DL and FOH are combined as conversion because they enter at the same rate). The note stops here; later process-costing steps were not captured.

### Chapter 24: Performance evaluation of decentralized operations (class exercises, Nov 2022)
The file holds textbook exercise statements (the solutions are hidden behind "Answer" buttons):
- **DuPont ROI** = profit margin × investment turnover (income ÷ sales × sales ÷ assets); **residual income** = income from operations − minimum acceptable return on assets. Starwood example, recomputed by me: Hotel Ownership margin 13.0%, turnover 0.68, ROI 8.9%, residual income $249M at a 5% minimum; Vacation Ownership margin 15.3%, turnover 0.32, ROI 4.9%, residual income about **−$2M**.
- **Transfer pricing** (Dart Industries): outside purchases cost $180/unit; the Components Division has idle capacity at $125 variable cost; 40,000 units move. Recomputed: total company income rises **$2.2M** regardless of price; at a $145 transfer price Instrument gains $1.4M and Components $0.8M; at $158 they gain $0.88M and $1.32M. Under negotiated pricing the acceptable range is **$125 to $180**.
