# Public Speaking (COMM 1315)

Notes from UHCL **COMM 1315 – Public Speaking** (Spring 2022). Part of [[topics/uhcl-coursework]]. Topic ideas for speech 1 overlap with hobbies in [[topics/personal-and-hobbies]] and [[topics/dnd-campaigns-and-worldbuilding]] (TTRPGs).

## Core concepts

### Chapter 2 key terms
- **Dialogic** vs **monologic** communication, **relational** communication (finding the connection between self and world), **praxis**, and **communication anxiety**.
- Assignment: tell a 2-minute story about a memorable experience as a student.

### Chapter 3: Audience and care
- **Audience analysis** (observing who is listening); *speaking with* (collaborative) vs *speaking for* (passive education); **reflexivity** (acknowledging your place in communication); **ethic of care** (advocacy of care for the audience); **implicit bias** (stereotyping). Reflexivity and ethic of care go hand in hand. Prompt: what is my public-speaking persona?

### Chapter 4: Speech purposes
- **Ceremonial** (rituals: weddings, funerals, promotions), **demonstration** (teaching, hands-on), **epideictic** (awards, reflection, always framed around gratitude), **informative** (pure information, passive listener), **forensic** (about the past, framed as deliberation, e.g. courtroom), **deliberative** (about what hasn't happened: policy, proposals), **persuasive** (influence now).
- Public speaking is constantly **dialogic**: good speakers keep adjusting to the audience, and how we learned to speak is inherited from past experiences.

### Chapter 9: Meaning and passion
- **Denotative** (literal, dictionary) vs **connotative** (cloud of associations) meaning; **impassioned public speaking** (strong feeling meant to enact change); **invitational rhetoric** (encourages audience engagement); **rhetorical frame** (how a speaker focuses words for immediate needs). Passion is tied to rhetorical strategy.

### Chapter 10: Reasoning and fallacies
- Reasoning means showing listeners how you reached your conclusion. Types: **analogical** (shared experience), **situated** (particular times and places).
- Fallacies (weaknesses in logic): **ad populum** (believed because others believe it), **hasty generalization** (conclusion from inadequate evidence), **post-hoc** (one event followed another so it caused it), **slippery slope** (a chain of increasingly negative consequences from one event).

### Chapter 11: Communication rules and social media
- **Constitutive rules** say what a message means; **regulative rules** organize who/what/when/where/why of communication. **Slacktivism** = empty advocacy (talk without action).
- Reflections: social media is the next evolution of public communication; misinformation spreads faster than ever as communication speed approaches constant.

### Final notes (`Final Notes`)
- A study sheet of the terms above (ad populum, connotative/denotative meaning, analogical reasoning, fallacies, impassioned public speaking, …) for a final speech that **must be 2 minutes**.

### Speech 1: "I am an advocate for…"
- 3–5 minutes; hard-copy outline due the day of the speech; one 3×5 notecard allowed.
- Reflection prompts: why it matters to me, what others should know, and what action I'd hope the audience takes. An *advocacy* is something you like or care about and **actively participate** in.
- Brainstormed topics: Seinfeld, Venture Bros, cooking, Star Trek, Green Lantern, Spider-Man, TTRPGs.

### Speech 3: "Online vs face-to-face life"
- 4–5 minutes, 2–3 citations. Planned theme ("Meat Space"): the importance of face-to-face communication for childhood development (avoiding the social-skills angle), arguing that humans don't just crave in-person contact but physically need it.
