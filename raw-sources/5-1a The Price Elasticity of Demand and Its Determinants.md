# The Price Elasticity of Demand and Its Determinants
The *price elasticity of demand*[^1] measures how much the quantitiy demanded responds to a change in price. 
Demand for a good is said to be:
- **Elastic** if the quantity demanded responds substantially to changes in the price
- **Inelsatic** if the quantity demanded responds only slightly to changes in the price

Price elasticity of demand for any good measures how willing consumers are to buy less of the good as its price rises. Because a demand curve reflets the many economic, social and psychological forces that shape consumer preferences, there is no simple, universal rule for what determines a demand curve's elasticity. Based on past experience though, we can determine some rules of thumb.

## Availability of Close Substitutes
A good with close subs tends to have more elastic demand because it is easier for a consumer to switch from that good to another.
Ex. Butter and Margarine are substitutes, if the price of butter increases a small amount and margarines doesn't, the quantity demanded of butter will fall by a large amount.

Ex 2. Eggs have no real close sub. A small increase if the price of eggs would not cause any sizeable drop in the amount of eggs demanded.

## Necessities vs Luxuries
Necessities are typically inelastic, luxuries are typically elastic.
What determines necessity vs luxury isn't intrinsic to the good, but based on buyer's preference.

## Definition of the Market
Elasticity of demand depends on how we draw the boundaries of a market. A narrow defined market will have more elastic demand than a broadly defined market, simply becuase a broad market has more subs available.

Ex. Food as a market has relatively inelastic demand, Ice cream as a market has higher elasticity because of the various different subs for flavors and brands.

## Time Horizon
Goods have more elastic demand over a longer timeline. When the price of gas rises, quantity demanded of gasoline will only fall slightly at first, but as time wears on more people will bike or use public transport, use a fuel efficient car, or move closer to work. Within a few years the demanded quantity of gas has fallen substantially.
