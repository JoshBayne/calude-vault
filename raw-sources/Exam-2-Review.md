[LT Chapter 13]{.underline}

- Pollution for water -- unique?
  - Recreational benefits -- more important for water than air
  - Large economies of scale -- sewage and waste treatment plants create a possibility of large centralized treatment facilities
  - Causes of water pollution difficult to trace
- Types of waste receiving water
  - Surface water -- river, lakes, oceans
    - Historically policies focus on cleaning-up and preventing pollution
  - Groundwater -- water beneath earths surface in soil, rocks, geological formations that are saturated
    - Once pristine, shown to be subject to considerable toxic contamination
  - Oceans -- largest section of surface water on earth
    - Many uses, recreational vs practical
- Sources of contamination
  - Point sources -- pollution that discharges into surface waters at specific location like a pipe or ditch
  - Nonpoint sources -- pollution that affects water in more indirect of diffuse way
    - Runoff from pesticides
    - Harder to control because source and timing difficult to predict, little legislation till now
  - Nonpoint sources compose over half of waste load borne by US Waters
  - Contamination of groundwater -- pollution leeches into water saturated region
    - Many pollutants can be naturally filtered by rocks and soil
    - Toxic chemicals usually not filtered, hard to do manually, water doesn't naturally have high replenishing stock to mix in
  - Ocean pollution primary sources
    - Oil spills
    - Ocean dumping
    - trash
- Types of pollutants
  - Fund pollutants -- environment has some assimilative capacity, if absorption is higher than discharge rate, will not accumulate
    - Degradable - breaks into component parts in water
      - Usually organic
      - Monitor ambient conditions of water
      - Monitor magnitude of emissions
    - Thermal pollution -- caused when industrial or electrical plant uses surface water as coolant, returning heated water
      - Can raise temps, reduce oxygen, change ecology
    - Eutrophic -- excess nitrogen and phosphorus in water, stimulates algae and weed growth, odor, bad taste, aesthetic problems
  - Stock pollutants -- accumulate in environment, no natural process to remove or transform them, water cannot clean itself of them
    - Inorganic chemicals and minerals
      - Cadmium, lead, mercury
    - Accumulate in food chain and contaminate food
      - Fish
      - Rice and beans
    - Mercury dumping in japan -- accumulated in fish and consumed 3x a day (52 dead, 150+ nerve damage)
- Pollution control history
  - Traditional
    - 1899 refuse act -- prevented discharge into rivers and lakes for navigation
    - 1948 water pollution control act -- first federal attempt to control, allowed feds to conduct investigations, research and surveys. Main control still at state
    - 1958 WPCA amendments
      - Fed support for water waste treatment plant construction
      - Direct fed regulation of waste discharge
    - 1965 Water Quality Act -- ambient water standards for interstate watercourses
    - 1975 clean water act -- eliminate discharge in navigable waters by 1985, increased water quality to protect wildlife by 1983
    - 1977 CWA Amendments -- distinction between conventional and toxic pollutants
      - Extended all deadlines of original CWA
    - 1986 Safe Drinking Water Act -- Requires standards for contaminants, set standards based on "best available economically achievable", monitor public water for contaminants
    - 2007 -- regulation on copper and lead piping
    - 2017 Clean Water Rule -- expand protection of large water bodies to wetland and tributaries (many loopholes)
  - Nonpoint Pollution
    - 1987 Clean Water Act -- authorized 400m to program to help states control runoff, left states responsible
    - Since 80s focus on nonpoint increased
      - Removed 40-45M acres of erodible land from cultivation -- subsidies to farmers to plant trees and grass
      - Separate stormwater from sewage, during rainfall no overflow from sewage treatment plants
    - Nonpoint control low because costs high and mentality is marginal damages are smaller (false)
- Unintended consequences
  - Policy leakage -- grocery bags banned so buy trash bags instead
- When does control work? Examples
  - Bag ban / tax

[Price Structure Review]{.underline}

- Cost structures
  - Production cost table
  - Cost equations
    - Total Cost = Variable Cost + Fixed Cost
    - Avg Variable Cost = Variable Cost / Quantity
    - Avg Fixed Cost = Fixed Cost / Quantity
    - Avg Total Cost = Total Cost / Quantity
    - Avg Total Cost = Avg Fixed Cost + Avg Variable Cost
    - Marginal Cost = Change in Total Cost / Change in Quantity
  - Shutdown vs stay open (at profit or loss) rules
    - Short Run
      - Reduce VC to 0
      - Firm still pays FC (leases, rent)
      - Shutdown when paying FC is cheaper than operating and losing money on VC
    - Shutdown if Market price is less than minimum avg variable cost
    - Stay Open - Market Price >= Minimum avg variable cost
  - Cost curve graph -- important points
    - Profit Maximization -- MR = MC = P
    - Above ATC Line, Profit Zone
    - ATC = MC -- 0 Profit Point
    - MC between AC and AVC -- Loss but operate in short run
    - AVC = MC -- Shutdown point
    - MC Below AVC - shutdown
