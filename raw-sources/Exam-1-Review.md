- Positive vs normative statements
  - Positive statement - world as it is. Verifiable.
  - Normative statement - world as it should be, not verifiable.
- Static efficiency
  - Chief normative economic criterion for choosing amongst various outcomes happening at same time.
  - Surplus derived from resources is maximized by allocation.
- Property rights -- why should we need them, characteristics
  - Defines owners' rights, limitations, privileges for a resource
  - Can be for ind, groups, state
  - Exclusivity, Transferability, Enforceability
- Externalities -- negative, graphed
  - Negative externality - neg effect of market exchange on a third party who is external to the exchange.
    - Ex. Neighors barking dog
    - Shifts supply line to the left, creates DWL
- Supply/Demand
  - CS, PS, TS, DWL
  - CS - above equilibrium price inside line
  - PS - Below equilibrium price inside line
  - TS- Combination of both surpluses
  - DWL - Loss in efficiency

[LT Chapter 5]{.underline}

- Types of value in the environment
  - Intrinsic - independent of human interest
  - Instrumental - derived from its usefulness in stisfying humans
- Types of methods for measuring value
  - Revealed Pref
  - Direct - Market price simulated markets - create a model to sim human behavior and choices
  - Indirect - Travel cost - infer how much visitors willing to pay to visit
  - Indirect - Hedonic property - use market data to break down prices into attributes, identify how env affects pricing
  - Indirect - Hedonic wage - isolating risky env for workers
  - Indirect - Avoidance expenditure - how much willing to pay to avoid an env
    - Stated Pref
  - Direct - Contingent Valuation - Ask what what be willing to pay for outcome
  - Indirect - Choice experiments - give set of options and let them pick based on given information



[D Chapter 4]{.underline}

- Why does the government get involved with markets?
  - Affect markets for better outcomes
- How does government control prices?
  - Price controls (price ceiling, price floor)
    - Ceiling - price lower than equilibrium
    - Floor - price set higher than equilibrium
  - Tax/Subsidy
    - Tax - cost to engage in activity
    - Subsidy - money given to produce more
- Feed-in tariffs
  - Rates higher than other electricity sources
  - Cost more than power from non-renewables
  - Utilities able to pass feed-in tariff cost to all customers, not just renewable users
  - Inelastic demand
- Types of taxes
  - Severance Tax - Tax on producing resource, "severing" from the earth
  - Ad valorem tax - tax as a percent of price rather than constant per unit
- Modeling taxes in a competitive market
  - Who pays the taxes? Consumers? Producers?
    - Both. Costs of a tax pass along to both.
