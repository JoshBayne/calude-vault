# Computing the Price Elasticity of Demand
Economists compute the price elasticity of demand as the percentage change in the quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in the price
$$Price\phantom{0}Elasticity\phantom{0}of\phantom{0}Demand=\frac{Percentage\phantom{0}Change\phantom{0}in\phantom{0}Quantity\phantom{0}Demanded}{Percentage\phantom{0}Change\phantom{0}in\phantom{0}Price}$$
For example, suppose that a 10 percent increase in ice-ceram cone prices causes the amount of ice cream you buy to fall 20 percent. We calculate the elasticity of demand as
$$Price\phantom{0}Elasticity\phantom{0}of\phantom{0}Demand=\frac{20\%}{10\%}=2$$
In this example, the elasticity is 2, reflecting that the change in the quantity demanded is proportionally twice as large as the change in the price.

Since quantity demanded of a good is negatively related to the price, the percent change in quantity will always have the opposite sign as the percent change in price. In this example, the percentage change in price was positive 10 percent (to refelct and increase), and the percent change in quantity was negative 20 percent (to reflect a decrease). Because of this, sometimes elasticities of demand are reported as negative numbers. Commonly though the sign is dropped altoghether to report an absolute value. With this convention, a larger price elasticity implies a greater responsiveness of quantity demanded to change in price.
