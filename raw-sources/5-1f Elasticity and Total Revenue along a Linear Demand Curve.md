# Elasticity and Total Revenue along a Linear Demand Curve
We know that a straight line has a constant slope. Slope is rise over run, which here is the ration of the change in price ("rise") to the change in quantity("run"). This particular demand curves slope is constant because each 1 dollar increase in price causes the same two-unit decrease in quantity demanded.

 Even though the slope of a linear demand curve is constant, elasticity is not. This is true because the slop is the ratio of *changes* in the two variables, whereas the elasticity is the ratio of *percent changes* in the two variables. The table above uses the midpoint method to calculate the price elasticity of demand and illustrates *At points with a low price and high quantity, the demand curve is inelastic. At points with a high price and low quantity, the demand curve is elastic.*

The explanation for this fact comes from the arithmetic of percent changes. When the price is low and consumers are buying a lot, 1 price increase and two-unit reduction in quantity demanded constitutes a large percent increase in the price and a small percent decrease in the quantity demanded. resulting in a small elasticity. When the price is high and consumers are not buying much, the same 1 price increase  and two unit reduction in quantity demanded constitutes a small percentage increase in the price and a large percentage decrease in the quantity demanded, resulting in large elasticity.

The table also present total revenue at each point on the demand curve. These numbers illustrate the relationship between total revenue and elasticity.When the price is 1, for instance, demand is inelastic and a price increase to 2 raises total revenue. When the price is 5, demand is elastic and a price increase to 6 reduces total revenue. Between 3 and 4, demand is exactly unit elasic and total revenue is the same at these two prices.

The linear demand curve illustrates that the price elasticity of demand need not be the same at all points on a demand curve. A constant elasticity is possible, but it is not always the case, and it is never the case for a linear demand curve.
