The Profit a firm makes on its product is the difference between the amount it receives from sales (revenue) and its cost. if x units are produced and sold, the profit P(x) is determined by the revenue R(x) and the cost C(x) by
P(x)=R(x)-C(x)
Revenue is fouind by multiplying the price per unit by the number of units.
R(x) = (price per unit)(number of units)
One point that is always on the revenue curve is R(0)=0. If no units are sold, then there is no revenue. Cost is found by adding the variable costs (VC) to the fixed costs (FC),
C(x)=VC+FC
Variable costs depend on the number of units produced, while the fixed costs are cosntant regardless of the number of units produced. If no units are produced, then there are no variable costs, so C(0)=FC. Therefore, one point we know on profit is P(0)=0-FC=-FC

we often examine the rate at which revenue, cost, and proft are increasing or decreasing. When the functions are linear, the rates are the slopes of the lines. For example, if cost in dollars to produce x units is C(x)=500+15x, then the rate at which the slopes of the lines. For example, if cost in dollars to produce x units is C(x)=500+15x, then the rate at which cost is increasing is 15/unit. This is called the marginal cost written MC(line over this). If we say that MC=15, that means every addition unit produced will increase the total cost by 15. Similarly, MR is the marginal revenue, and MP is the marginal profit.

A very important point oj the graph of revenue and cost is the break-even point. This is the point where revenue is equal to cost (profit is zero). Before the break even point, cost is greater than revenue and profit is negative. After the break even point (at least for a while), revenue is greater than cost and profit is positive.

Another intersection point that concerns us is the market equilibrium. This occurs when the supply function intersects with the demand function. The law of supply states that the quantity supplied for sale will increase as the price of a product increases. The law of demand states that the quantity demanded will decrease as the price increases.
