What weekly tasks should I be doing to ensure full accounts payable compliance within a company

Here are the essential weekly accounts payable compliance tasks that should be performed:

**Invoice Processing and Verification**  
Review and verify all incoming invoices for accuracy, proper authorization, and compliance with purchase orders or contracts. Ensure invoices contain required information like vendor details, itemized charges, and proper tax calculations. Flag any discrepancies or missing documentation for resolution.

**Three-Way Matching**  
Perform three-way matching between purchase orders, receiving reports, and invoices to ensure goods or services were actually received and match what was ordered and invoiced. This prevents duplicate payments and unauthorized purchases.

**Approval Workflow Monitoring**  
Review pending approvals in your system to ensure invoices are moving through the proper authorization hierarchy. Follow up on bottlenecks and ensure approvers are adhering to delegation of authority policies and spending limits.

**Vendor Master File Maintenance**  
Review new vendor setup requests and existing vendor information for accuracy. Verify tax ID numbers, banking details, and ensure proper vendor classification for tax reporting. Remove or flag inactive vendors to prevent fraudulent payments.

**Payment Authorization Review**  
Examine payment runs before execution to verify accuracy of amounts, payees, and payment methods. Ensure payments align with approved invoices and contract terms, including early payment discounts where applicable.

**Segregation of Duties Compliance**  
Monitor that different individuals are handling invoice entry, approval, and payment processing. Review system access logs to ensure no single person has inappropriate access to multiple stages of the AP process.

**Reporting and Analytics**  
Generate and review weekly AP aging reports, duplicate payment reports, and exception reports. Analyze key metrics like days payable outstanding and identify trends that might indicate process breakdowns or compliance issues.

**Documentation and Audit Trail**  
Ensure all transactions have proper supporting documentation and maintain complete audit trails. Review electronic workflows and approvals to confirm they meet internal controls and external audit requirements.

These weekly reviews help maintain strong internal controls, prevent fraud, ensure accurate financial reporting, and prepare for both internal and external audits.

How would one collect the information necessary for a Vendor Master File

Here’s how to systematically collect the information needed for a comprehensive Vendor Master File:

**Initial Vendor Information Package**  
Create a standardized vendor setup form or questionnaire that captures all required data upfront. This should include basic company information, contact details, banking information, tax documentation, and business classifications. Send this package to new vendors before any business relationship begins.

**Tax Documentation Collection**  
Obtain Form W-9 from domestic vendors or appropriate W-8 forms from foreign vendors to collect Taxpayer Identification Numbers (TIN) or Social Security Numbers. For international vendors, gather additional tax treaty documentation if applicable. Verify these numbers through IRS databases when possible.

**Banking and Payment Information**  
Collect complete banking details including bank name, routing numbers, account numbers, and account type. For ACH payments, obtain voided checks or bank letters confirming account ownership. For international vendors, gather SWIFT codes and correspondent banking information.

**Business License and Registration Verification**  
Request copies of business licenses, articles of incorporation, or partnership agreements. Verify the vendor’s legal business name and structure through state databases or commercial verification services. Confirm the business is in good standing and authorized to operate.

**Insurance and Bonding Documentation**  
Collect certificates of insurance showing general liability, professional liability, and workers’ compensation coverage as required by your contracts. Obtain bonding information if applicable. Ensure your company is listed as an additional insured where appropriate.

**Credit and Financial Assessment**  
Run credit reports through commercial credit agencies like Dun & Bradstreet or Experian Business. Request financial statements for significant vendors. Review trade references from other companies the vendor works with to assess payment history and reliability.

**Compliance and Regulatory Information**  
Gather any required certifications such as minority or women-owned business certifications, environmental compliance documentation, or industry-specific licenses. For government contractors, collect System for Award Management (SAM) registration information.

**Contact and Communication Setup**  
Establish primary and secondary contacts for different functions like invoicing, customer service, and technical support. Set up preferred communication methods and escalation procedures. Collect emergency contact information for critical vendors.

**Internal Approval and Documentation**  
Route the completed vendor package through your internal approval process, typically including procurement, legal, and finance teams. Document the business justification for the vendor relationship and maintain all supporting documentation in both physical and electronic formats.

**Ongoing Maintenance Process**  
Establish procedures for regularly updating vendor information, typically annually or when significant changes occur. Set up automated reminders for insurance renewals, contract expirations, and required documentation updates.

The key is creating a standardized, repeatable process that ensures all necessary information is collected before vendor setup and maintained throughout the business relationship.
