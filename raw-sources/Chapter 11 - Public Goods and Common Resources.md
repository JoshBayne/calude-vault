## Introduction
We consume goods without paying:
Parks, national defense, clean air and water.
When goods have no prices the market forces that normally allocate resources are absent.
The private market may fail to provide the socially efficient quantity of such goods

Governments can sometimes improve market outcomes

### Excludability
Property of a good whereby a person can be prevented from using it.

### Rivalry in consumption
Property where one persons use diminishes other peoples use.

### The different kinds of goods
1. Private goods - Excludable and rival in consumption (food)
2. Public Goods - Not excludable and not rival in consumption (national defense)
3. Common resources - Rival in consumtion and not excludable (fish in the ocean)
4. Club Goods - Excludable and not rival in consumption (cable)

### Tragedy of the commons
parable the shows why common resources are used more than desireable
Medieval town where sheep graze on common land
As the pop gorws the number of sheep grows
Amount of land is fixed, grass gets over grazed by sheep

Social and private incentives differ
- The private incentives like using land for free outweigh the social incentives like using it carefully
Arises because of negatives externalities
- Allowing ones flock to graze on the common land redces its quality for other families
People neglect this external cost, resulting in overuse of land

## Ways to avoid the tragedy of the commons
Impose corrective taxes on the use of the resource to "internalize the externality"
regulate use of resource "command and control approach"
Auction of permits to allow use of the land
Divide the resource, sell off lots to individuals. Each owner will have incentive to not over use resource
