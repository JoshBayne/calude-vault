# Other Demand Elasticities

In addition to the price elasticity of demand, economists use other elasticities to describe the behavior of buyers in a market.

## Income Elasticity of Demand
The *income elasticity of demand* measures how the quantity demanded changes as consumer income changes. It is calculated as the percentage change in quantity demanded divided by the percentage change in income
$$
Income\phantom{0}Elasticity\phantom{0}of\phantom{0}Demand={Percentage\phantom{0}change\phantom{0}in\phantom{0}quantity\phantom{0}demanded\over Percentage\phantom{0}change\phantom{0}in\phantom{0}income}
$$
Most goods are *normal goods*: Higher income raises the quantity demanded.Because quantity demanded and income both move together, normal goods have positive income elasticities. Some goods, such as bus rides, are *inferior goods*: Higher income lowers the quantity demanded. Because quantity demanded and income move opposite, inferior goods have negative income elasticities.

Even among normal goods, income elasticities vary substantially in size. Necessities such as food tend to have small income elasticities becuase these willl normally be bought even when incomes are low. 
- *Engel's Law* - As a family's income rises, the percent of its income spent on food declines, indicating an income elasticity less than one.
By contrast, luxuries like jewelry tend to have large income elasticities because consumers feel that they don't need these items if their incomes are low.

## Cross-Price Elasticity of Demand
The *cross-price elasticity of demand* measures how the quantity demanded of one good responds to a change in the price of another good. It is calculated as the percentage change in quantity demanded of good one divided by the percentage change in the price of good two.

$$
Cross-Price\phantom{0}elasticity\phantom{0}of\phantom{0}demand={Percentage\phantom{0}change\phantom{0}in\phantom{0}quantity\phantom{0}demanded\phantom{0}of\phantom{0}good\phantom{0}one\over Percentage\phantom{0}change\phantom{0}in\phantom{0}the\phantom{0}price\phantom{0}of\phantom{0}good\phantom{0}two}
$$

Whether the cross-price elasticity is positive or negative depends on whther the two goods are substitutes or complements.
- Substitutes, cross-price elasticity is positive
- Complements, corss-price elasticity is negative
