## Terminology – different types of opinions
- Unanimous - All judges agree
- Majority - Not unanimous, outlines the views of the majority of judges
- Concurring - Judge who agree with majority, but has different reasoning
- Dissenting - views of one or more judges who disagree with majority
- Plurality - At least 5 judges agree on result, but not on a single rationale or opinion being a majority
- Per Curiam - opinion that doesn't indicate who wrote it

## Stare decisis
"to stand on decided cases"
Practice of deciding new cases based on precedent from previous cases
Higher court decisions are binding on lower courts
Judges are obligated to follow precedents established in jurisdictions
Only compelling reason should allow for overturning of precedent

## Case of first impression
No precedent
Courts may refer to persuasive authorities, public policy, or widely held socail values in crafting an opinion

## Types of personal jurisdiction
In personam (people)
- jurisdiction gives court power to compel the presence of the parties (including corps) to appear before the court and litigate
In rem (property)
- Jurisdiction gives court power to exercise jurisdiction over property (real, personal, tangible, or intangible) within its boundaries
Under authority of a states long arm statues, a court can exercise personal jurisdiction on certain out-of-state defendants
- Minimum contacts - defendant has sufficent connection to state for exercise of power
- corporate contacts - business does intentional commerce in the state

## Original v. appellate jurisdiction
Original - courts where lawsuit began, trial takes place, and evidence is presented

## Federal and state court structure
Federal courts have exclusice jurisdiction over certain types of cases
- federal crimes
- bankruptcy
- most patent and copyright cases
- any lawsuit against the US
- some areas of admiralty law
State courts have exclusive jurisdiction over cases concerning state law, like divorce and adoption
Federal and state courts have concurrent jurisdiction when both have power to hear a case (ex. diversity of citizenship cases)

## Commerce clause
Grants national govt power to regulate interstate commerce
"Dormant" commerce clause
- fed govt has *exclusive* authority to regulate commerce that substantially affects trade among states (positive aspect)
- States do not have authorityto regulate interstate commerce (negative aspect)
Greater impact on business than any other provision
Gibbons v. Ogden - commerce within states could also be regulated by national govt as long as activies substantially affected interstate commerce
Courts weigh states interest in regulating a certain matter against the burden that regulation places on interstate commerce

## Federalism
Shared power between national and state governments
National has limited, enumerated powers from state
- Implied power to carry out necessary action to enfoce enumerated power
All other powers are expressly "reserved" to the states via 10th amendment

## Taxing and spending clause
Federal gov can collect taxes for payment of debts, and common defense and welfare of states

## Privileges and immunities clause
Citizens of one state are entitled to the privileges and immunities of citizens in the several states
no loss of rights for being out of state

## Bill of Rights
First 10 amendments
All apply to natural persons and most apply to business entities as well
limits federal AND state action
Incorporated to states via 14th amendment due process clause
neither states nor feds can deprive a person of those rights
Not absolute, SCOTUS gives meaning to them and determines boundaries

## Supremacy clause
When state and federal law conflict, federal law supercedes
when states and fed share power, fed law preempts state
often, federal statutes will include preemption provision to make congress intent clear

## Judicial review

## Disparagement of Property Torts
