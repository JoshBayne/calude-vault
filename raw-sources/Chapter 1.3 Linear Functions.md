A function f is a linear function if it can be written in the form f(x)=mx+b. This particular way of writing a line is slope-intercept form. We often also deal with vertical lines (x=a), but vertical lines are not funcitons.

The slope (or rate of change)  of a linear fuinction is m. The slope of a line tells us how many units the line goes up (if positive) or down (if negative) for every unit it moves to the right. If we do not know the equations of a line, but we have two points on the line, $(x_1,y_1)$ and $(x_2,y_2)$, then the slope of the line is
$$
m=\frac{change\phantom{0}in\phantom{0}y}{change\phantom{0}in\phantom{0}x}=\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}= \frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}
$$
*Horizontal line is a constant function, slope of 0*
*No slope = vertical line*

We already have the slope intercept form of a line. There is another common way we write a line called the point-slope form. If we find the slope of the line passing through the points (x,y) and (x1,y1) we get
(y-y1)/(x-x1)=m

multiplying both sides of the equations by x-x1 gives us the point-slope form of a line
y-y1=m(x-x1)
If we solve for y and simplify, we will have the slope intercept form of the line.

Consider 2 line y=m1x+b1 and y=m2x+b2. The two lines are **parallel** if m1=m2 (but b1 never equals b2). Parallel lines have the same slope and never intersect. The lines are **perpendicular** if m1=-(1/m2). Perpendicular lines have slopes that are negative reciprocals of each other (e.g 3/5 and -5/3) and intersect at right angles.
