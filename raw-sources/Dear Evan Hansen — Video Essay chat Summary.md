# Dear Evan Hansen — Video Essay Summary

**Working title:** "Dear Evan Hansen, You Deserved Consequences" **Thesis (your position):** Evan Hansen is an offensive representation of autism. The show is an offensive portrayal of mental illness and suicide. The plot contrivances give Evan no consequences, and that is infuriating. **Target runtime:** 30–34 minutes **Status:** Framework done. Sources listed below. Some items need a check before you script (see the last section).

---

## 1. Decisions from the committee and your answers

- **Autism claim is broadened.** The text never says Evan is autistic. It names social anxiety. So the essay does not say "he is autistic and it is wrong." It says the show borrows the look of disability and mental illness (autistic-coded traits, anxiety symptoms, therapy language) as one shorthand, and never examines any of it. Start Act 2 with this correction. Then a viewer cannot dismiss the claim in one sentence.
- **Consequences claim: "disproportionately mild."** Evan does lose things. He is exposed. He loses Zoe. His friendships with Jared and Alana break. The Murphy family cuts contact. The argument is that none of this matches months of lying to a grieving family. "Zero consequences" is too strong, and a viewer can prove it wrong.
- **Defenders get brief treatment.** One short, fair beat per claim, with a real answer. "Brief" does not mean "empty."
- **Care with suicide material.** Keep language clinical. Give no detail beyond what the argument needs. Slow the pace in Act 3.
- **Stay more precise than the show.** Do not blur "autistic-coded" and "socially anxious" in your own narration.

## 3. Sources used

These claims came from web searches. Source names are as the search results showed them. I did not keep the full URLs.

**Plot**

- Evan's therapist assigns letters to himself. Connor takes one. Connor dies by suicide. His family finds the letter and thinks it was his note to a close friend. Evan does not correct them. With Jared and Alana he builds The Connor Project. His anxiety seems to improve during the lie.

**Consequences**

- **Looper**, "Why Some Fans Think Dear Evan Hansen Is So Controversial." The lack of real consequences can read as saying that mentally unwell people should not face consequences. The show leans on mental-illness tropes instead of subverting them.
- **Defense: Ruthie Fierberg, "In Defense of Dear Evan Hansen" (Medium).** The creators decline to judge Evan in black and white. The audience holds sympathy and moral failure together. She also compares Evan to Mama Rose and Sweeney Todd, who get compassion without punishment. She calls the backlash an overcorrection.
- **OnStage Blog**, "From Hero to Villain: Why We Suddenly Hate Evan Hansen." How opinion shifted between the 2016 stage show and the 2021 film.

**Disability and coding**

- A detailed breakdown of Ben Platt's performance (Tumblr, "Evan Hansen is on the Autism Spectrum and Here's Why"): missed cues, no eye contact, a fixed interest in trees, stimming with hands or a backpack strap. Fan analysis. Use as a guide for your own viewing.
- An autistic writer's analysis: showing autistic traits without naming autism can add to the invisibility of autistic people. This raises a question of creator responsibility.
- A source on social anxiety: the size of Evan's lie can make viewers link social anxiety with manipulation or attention-seeking. That is not how the condition usually shows.
- The counterpoint: writers who aim for "awkward and nerdy" often copy autistic-coded habits by accident, because that is the cultural shorthand.

**Suicide and mental illness**

- **"the elysian collective," "Reflections on Dear Evan Hansen and Poor Representation" (Medium, The Startup).** A first-person review by an autistic writer who is also a suicide-attempt survivor. The show had a chance to show real responses and, in the writer's account, failed at almost every turn. Read the full piece. I saw only part of it.
- **Medium, "Media Portrayal of Mental Health: Dear Evan Hansen."** The posthumous-meaning point. A classmate admits he did not know Connor but regrets not trying. The "one person is enough" theme in "Disappear" and "You Will Be Found" is a shallow picture of depression. The original has a trigger warning.
- Creator Steven Levenson has said the aim was to let people know there is help and that people will listen. (Used for the brief defense.)

**Reception**

- The 2021 film got a 28% critics score on Rotten Tomatoes. Many critics faulted its mental-health portrayal.
- **Mel Valentin, "Dear Evan Hansen Disappoints on Practically Every Level" (Medium).** Harsh review of the film. Label as opinion.
