# Chart of Accounts
A group of accoutns for a business entity is called a *ledger*[^1]. A list of the accounts in the ledge is called a *chart of accounts*[^2]. The accounts are normally listed in the order in which they appear in the financial statements.

### Order of Statements
1. Balance Sheet Accounts
	1. Assets
	2. Liabilities
	3. Stockholders/Owners Equity
2. Income Statement
	1. Revenues
	2. Expenses

## Assets
*Assets*[^3] are resources owned by the business entity. Can be physical like cash or supplies, or intangible like patents, copyrights and trademarks. Also includes accounts receivable, prepaid expenses (like insurance), buildings, equipment and land.

## Liabilities
*Liabilities*[^4] are debts owed to creditors. Often identified by titles on sheets that include **payable**. Examples include accounts payable, notes payable, and wages payable. Cash received before services are delivered created a liability to perform said service. A future commitment like this is called **unearned revenues**. Examples of this include magazine subscriptions received by a publisher and tuition received at the beginning of a term by a college.

## Stockholders' Equity
*Stockholders' Equity*[^5] is the stockholders' right to assets of the business. Represented by the balance of *common stock*[^6] and *retained earnings*[^7] accounts. A *dividends*[^8] account represents distributions of earnings to stockholders.

## Revenues
*Revenues*[^9] are increases in assets and stockholders equity as a result of selling services or products to customers. Examples include fees earned, fares earned, commissions revenue, and rent revenue.

## Expenses
*Expenses*[^10] result from using up assets or consuming services in the process of generating revenues. Examples include wages expense, rent expense, utilities expense, supplies expense, and misc expense.


[^1]: A group of accounts for a business
[^2]: A list of the accounts in the ledger
[^3]: The resources owned by a business
[^4]: The rights of creditors that represent debts of the business
[^5]: The stockholders' rights to the assets in a corporation
[^6]: An account representing the ownership rights of investors in a corporation
[^7]: An account representing the net income retained in a corporation
[^8]: An account representing the distribution of a corporations earnings to stockholders
[^9]: Increases in owner's equity as a result of providing services or selling goods to customers
[^10]: Assets used up or services consumed in the process of generating revenues
