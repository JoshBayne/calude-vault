The hull was rattling around him. He could feel each rivet loosen, each boot screaming to keep grip, each weld beginning to shear. Still he dove on, further into the anomaly. Faces passed in his mind, those he had loved, those he had lost, those who meant everything to him. His heart was racing, but he felt calm, serene, in the moment and yet beyond the moment. Nothing else but this choice mattered anymore. 

The radio master onboard the vessel looked up from his station. There was a brief pause, a moment of recognition. 
"He's...singing."
"Say again?"
"Singing, sir, he's singing to himself."

He continued on, deeper into the anomaly, his small craft buffeted by the shockwaves emanating from deep within the core. He might be close enough now, he couldn't know, all the instrumentation readouts had long since fried. He wasn't even sure the data could be relayed back, but he activated the switch. The craft became impossible to control anymore, the deployment of his payload caused the ship to finally begin to break apart. He continued to sing. 

The stabilizers broke first, each shearing off one after the other. The craft began to tumble, no longer aerodynamic, it flailed helplessly looking for a current to ride on. In it's tumble, the ship quickly dropped in speed, the fuselage acting as a massive brake. The stress of which caused it to almost immediately break apart. In the cockpit, the canopy cracked, spider webbed across the entire surface, then after what felt like an eternity of tension, shattered. In an instant, the anomaly flooded the space that was once pressurized air, the force of which actually ejected him from his pilot seat.

He saw as the ship tore itself apart, saw in the distance his home, the main vessel watch helplessly as he fell deeper. He kept singing.

"I lost the feed, I don't have any biometrics, and the ships onboard diagnostics don't make any sense."
"Explain."
"The ship computer is saying it's in 100s of places at once, simultaneously, before completely cutting out. The data packages we got from the payload probes are so corrupted it'll take weeks to clean up anything readable."
"Any sign of anything, speak up."

He had stopped singing. He'd been falling for several hours now by he's best estimation. Surely this was some form of hell, just sitting there in space waiting for a curious astronaut to find it's way in. His thoughts continued that way for what felt like weeks. Months? Had it been more than a year? How was he even still alive? Suit oxygen had dropped less than 20% but that's impossible, he had maybe 30 minutes of reserve air. 

Space came rushing back. The stars didn't match.
