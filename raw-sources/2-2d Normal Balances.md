# Normal Balances
The sum of the increases in an account is usually equal to or greated than the sum of the decreaes in the account. Thus the *normal balance of an account*[^1] is either a debit or a credit depending on whether the increases in the account are recorded as debits or credits. For example, because asset accounts are increased with debits, asset accounts have normally debit balances. Likewise, liability accounts are normally credit balances.

Debits and credits are sometimes abbreviated to Dr. and Cr. for short.

When an account normally having a debit balance has a credit balance, or vice versa, an error my have occured or an unusual situation my exist. For example, a credit balance in the office equipment account could only result from an error. This is because a business cannot have more decerases than increases of office equipment. On the otherhand, a debit balance in an accounts payable account could be the result of an overpayment. ^aa5049

[^1]: The side of an account (debit or credit) in which the balance normally appears based on the type of account and whether it is increased by debits or credits.
