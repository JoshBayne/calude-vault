Monopolistic Competition and Oligopoly

Two Extremes
- Perfect competition: many firms selling identical products
- Monopoly: One firm
Imperfect cometition - in between extremes
- oligopoly: few sellers offer similar or identical products
- monopolistic competition: many firms sell similar but not identical products

Monopolistic competition
- Characteristics
	- many sellers
	- product differentiation
		- not price takers, downward sloping D curve
	- Free entry and exit
		- zero economic profit in long run
- Examples
	- apartments, books, bottled water, clothing, fast food, clubs

|                           | Perfect           | monopolistic   | monopoly                  |
| ------------------------- | ----------------- | -------------- | ------------------------- |
| # of sellers              | many              | Many           | One                       |
| free entry/exit           | yes               | yes            | no                        |
| long run economic profits | Zero              | zero           | positive                  |
| products firm sell        | Identical         | differentiated | no close subs             |
| firm has market power?    | Nono- Price taker | yes            | yes                       |
| D curve facing firm       | Horizontal        | downward slope | downward slope (market D) |

Short run equilibrium
Profit maximization in the short run for a monopolstic competitive firm
- produice where MR = MC
- price: on the demand curve
- if P > ATC : profit
- if P < ATC Losses
- similar to monopoly

Long run equilibrium
If monopolistic competitive firms are making profit in short run
- new firms Incentive to enter the market
	- increase number of products
- reduces demand faced by each firm
	- curve shifts left, prices fall
- Each firms profit declines to zero
If losses in short run
- some firms exit, remaining firms enjoy higher demand and prices

A monopolistsic competitor in the long run
Entry and exit occurs until P = ATC and profit = zero
Firm charges a markup of price over marginal cost and does not produce at minimum ATC

Why monopolistic competition is less efficient than perfect competition
- Monopolistic competition
	- excess capacity : quantitiy is not at minimum ATC (it is on the downward sloping portion of ATC)
	- Markup over marginal cost: P > MC
- Perfect competition
	- quantity: at minimum ATC (efficient scale)
	- P = MC

Welfare of society
- monopolistically competitive markets
	- do not have all the desireable welfare properties of perfectly competitive markets
- sources of inefficiency
	- markup of price over marginal cost
	- too much or too little entry (number of firms in the market)
		- product variety externality
		- business stealing externality
- Markup P > MC
	- market quantity < socially efficent quantity
		- deadweight loss of monopoly pricing
	- product variety externality
		- consumers get extra surplus from the introduction of new products
	- busniess stealing externality
		- losses incurred by existing firms when new firms enter the market

Advertising
- Incentive to advertise
	- when firms sell differentiated products and charge prices above marginal cost
	- advertise to attract more buyers
- Advertising spending
	- highly differentiated goods : 10-20% of revenue
	- industrial products: very little advertising
	- homogenous products: no advertising
- in monopolisticall competitive industries
	- product differentiation and markup pricing lead naturally to the use of advertising
- the more differentiated
	- the more advertising purchased
- economists disagree about the social value of advertising
	- waste of resources?
	- valuable purpose?
- advertising as a signal of quality
	- little apparent information
	- real information offered - a signal
		- willing to spend large = signal about quality of product
	- content of advertising irrelevant

Critique of advertising
- firms advertise to manipulate
	- psychological rather than informational
		- creates desires that might not exist
- advertising impedes competition
	- increases perception of product differentiation
		- foster brand loyalty- higher markup
		- makes buyers less concerned with prices among similar goods

Defense of advertising
- the defense of advertising
	- provides useful information to buyers
	- informed buyers can more easily find and exploit price differences
	- advertising promotes competition and reduces market power
- results of one study
	- eyeglasses more expensive in states that blocked advertising vs states that allowed advertising

Brand names
- in many markets there are brand names and off brands
- brand names
	- spend more on advertising and charge more than generics
- As with advertising there is disagreement about the economics of brand names

Critics of brand names
- Products no differentiated
- irrationality: consumers are willing to pay more for brand names

Defense of brand names
- consumers - information about quality
- firms - incentive to maintain high quality to protect reputation of the brand name

Measuring market concentration
- concentration ratio
	- percentage of total output supplied by the four largest firms
	- higher ratio means less competition

Oligopoly
- market structure in which only a few sellers offer similar or identical priducts
- strategic behavior
	- firms decision about P or Q can affect other firms and cause them the react
	- the firms will consider these reactions when making decisions
- Game theory - the study of how people have in strategic situations

Duopoly
- one possible outcome: collusion
- collusion
	- agreement among firms in a market about quantities to produce or prices to charge
	- att and verizon could agree to produce half of the monopoly output
- cartel:
	- a group of firms acting in unison

Collusion vs self interest
- Both firms would be better off if both stick to the cartel agreement
	- but each firm has incentive to renege on this agreement
	- lesson: it is difficult for oligopoly firms to form cartels and honor their agreements

Equilibrium for an oligopoly
- Nash Equilibrium
	- Economic actors interacting with one another, each choose their best strategy
	- given the strategies that all other actors have chosen
- when firms in an oligopoly individually choose production to max profit
	- Produce Q
		- greater than monopoly Q
		- Less than competitive Q
	- Price
		- less than monopoly P
		- higher than competitive P = MC

The output and price effects
- Increasing output has two effects on a firms profits
	- Output effect: If P > MC, increasing output raises profits
	- Price effects:
		- Raising output increases market quantity, which reduces prices and reduces profit on all units sold

Size of the oligopoly
- as the number of sellers increases
	- price effect becomes smaller
	- oligopoly looks more and more like a competitive market
	- P approaches MC
	- Market quantity approaches socially efficient quantity
- Another benefit of international trade

Economics of Cooperation
- Prisoners dilemma
	- "game" between two captured prisoners
	- illustrates why cooperation is difficult to maintain even when it is mutually beneficial
- Dominant strategy
	- strategy that is best for a player in a game
	- regardless of what the other player chooses as a strategy'

Oligopolies as a prisoners dilemma
- When oligopolies form a cartel
	- in hopes f reaching monopoly level outcome, they become players in a prisoners dilemma
- Earlier duopoly example
	- att and verizon are duopolists
	- Cartel outcome maxs profits
	- each firm will choose to produce more though and overall everyone will make less than if they had colluded

Welfare of society
- nocooperative oligopoly equilbrium
	- May be bad for oligopolists
		- prevents them from achieving monopoly profits
	- may be bad for society
		- examples: Arms races, common resource usage
	- May be good for society
		- quantity and price move closer to optimal level

Why people sometimes cooperate
- when the game is repeated several times, cooperation may be possible
- Two strategies lead to cooperation
	- if your rival reneges in one round, you renege in all subsequent rounds
	- tit for tat
		- whatever your rival does in round one, you do the same in the following round

Public policy with oligopolies
- governments
	- can sometimes improve market outcomes
- policy makers
	- try to induce firms in an oligopoly to compete rather than to cooperate
	- move the allocation of resources closer to the social optimum
- Antitrust laws
	- sherman antitrust act of 1890
		- elevated agreements among oligopolists from an unenforceable contract to a criminal conspiracy
	- Clayton act of  1914
		- further strengthened the antitrust laws
	- used to prevent mergers
	- used to prevent oligopolists from colluding

Controversies over antitrust policy
- most people agree that price-fixing agreements among competitors should be illegal
- some economists are concerned that policymakers go too far when using antitrust laws to stifle business practices that are not necessarily harmful, and may have legit objectives

Resale price maintenance ("Fair Trade")
-  a manufacturer imposes lower limits on the prices retailers can charge
	- often opposed because it appears to reduce competition at the retail level
	- yet any market power the manufacturer has is at the wholesale level
		- no gains from restricting competition at the retail level
	- legit objective: prevent discount retailers from free-riding on the services provided by full-service retailers

Predatory pricing
- A firm cuts prices to prevent entry or drive a competitor out of the market
	- so that it can charge monopoly prices later
- Illegal under antitrust laws
	- difficult: when a price cut is predatory and when is it competitive and beneficial to consumers
- Many economists doubt that predatory pricing is rational
	- it involves selling at a loss
	- can backfire

Tying
- manufacturer budles two products together and sells them for a single price
- Critics
	- tying gives firms more market power by connecting weak products to strong ones
- Others: tying cannot change market power
	- buyers are not willing to pay more for two goods together than for the goods seperately
- firms may use tying for price discrimination
	- sometimes increases economic efficiency
