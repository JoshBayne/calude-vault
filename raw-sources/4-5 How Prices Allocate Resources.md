## How Prices Allocate Resources
Markets are *usually* a good way to organize economic activity
In market economies
- Prices adjust to balance supply and demand
The equilibrium prices
- Are the signals that guide economic decisions and thereby allocate scarce resources


## Summary
Economists use the model of supply and demand to analy competitive markets
- Many buyers and sellers, all are price takers

The demand curve shows how the quantity of a good demanded depends on the price
- Law of demand: As the price of a good falls, the quantity demanded rises, $D$ curve slopes downward

Other determinants of demand: income, prices of substitutes and complements, tastes, expectations, and number of buyers
- If one of these factors changes the $D$ curve shifts

The supply curve shows how the quantity supplied of a good depends on the price.
- Lat of supply: as the price of a good rises, the quantity supplied rises. $S$ curve slopes upward

Other determinants of supply : input prices, technology, expectations, and number of sellers.
- if one of these changes, the $S$ curve will shift

The intersection of the supply and demand curves determines the market equilibrium
- At the equilibrium price, quantity demanded=quantity supplied

The behavior of buyers and sellers naturally drives markets toward their equiliberium
- When the market price is above the equilibrium price, there is a surplus, which causes the market price to fall
- When the market price is below the equilibrium price, there is a shortage, which causes the market price to rise.

To analyze how any event influences a market, we use the supply-and-demand diagram to examine how the event affects the equilibrium price and quantity
- 1. Decide whether the even shifts the supply curve or the demand curve (or both)
- 2. Decide in which direction the curve shifts
- 3. Compare the new equilibrium with the initial one

In Market economies, prices are the signals that guide economic decisions and thereby allocate scarce resources.
