# Errors Affecting the Trial Balance
If the trial balance totals are not equal, an error has occured. In this case, the error must be found and corrected. A method useful in discovering errors is as follows:
	1. If the difference between the Debit and Credit column totals is 10,100,or 1000, an error in addition may have occured. In this case, re-add the trial balance column totals. If the error still exists, recompute the account balances.
	2. If the difference between the Debit and Credit column totals can be evenly divisible by 2, the error may be due to the entering of a debit balance as a credit or vice versa. In this case, review the trial balance  for account balances of one-half the difference that may have been entered in the wrong column. For example, if the Debit column total is $20,460 and the credit column total is $20236, the difference of $404 ($20,640-$20,236) may be due to a credit account balance of $202 that was entered as a debit account balance.
	3. If the difference between the Debit and Credit column totals is evenly divisible by 9, trace the account balances back to the ledger to see if an account balance was incorrectly copies from the ledger. Two common types of copying errors are transpositions and slides. a *transposition*[^1] occurs when the order of the digits is copies incorrectly, such as writing %542 as $452 or $524. In a *slide*[^2], the entire number is copied incorrectly one or more spaces to the right or left, such as writing $524.00 as $54.20 or $5420.00. In both cases, the resulting error will be evenly divisible by 9
	4. If the difference between Debit and crdit columns totals is not evenly divisible by 2 or 9, review the ledger to see if an account balance in the amount of the error has been omitted from the trial balance. If an error is not discovered, review the journal postings to see if a posting of a debit or credit may have been omitted.
	5. If an error is not discovered by the preceding steps, the accounting process must be retraces, beginning with the last journal entry.
The trial balance does not provide complete proof of the accuracy of the ledger. It indicates only that debits and credits are equal. This proof is of value, however, because errors often affect the equality of debits and credits.
