## Supply and Demand Together
**Equilibrium** is when the Price has reached a level where quantity supplied equals quantity demanded
**Equilibrium price**: Price where Q supplied = Q demanded
**Equilibrium quantity**: Q supplied and demanded at the equilibrium price
```chart
type: line
labels: [0,5,10,15,20,25,30]
series:
  - title: Demand
    data: [6,5,4,3,2,1,0]
  - title: Supply
    data: [0,1,2,3,4,5,6]
tension: 0.2
width: 100%
labelColors: false
fill: false
beginAtZero: true
```
Equilibirum price in example above is $3.00 at 15 units

### Surplus
Quantity supplied is greater than the quantity demanded
Using the chart above, if $P= \$5$, then $Q^D=5$ units and $Q^S=25$ units, resulting in a surplus of 20 units.
On a chart it is above the equilibrium price 
Facing a surplus, sellers try to increase sales by cutting the price, this causes $Q^D$ to rise and $Q^S$ to fall. Prices will continue to fall until the market reaches equilibrium

### Shortage
Quantity demanded is greater than the quantity supplied. Below the equilibrium price on a chart.
So, again using the chart above, if $P=\$1$ then $Q^D=25$ units and $Q^S=5$ units. Giving a shortage of 20 units overall.
Facing a shortage, sellers raise the price, cause $Q^D$ to fall and $Q^S$ to rise, which will reduce the shortage. Prices will continue to rise until the market reaches equilibrium.
