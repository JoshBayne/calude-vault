Demand is *elastic* if the quantity demanded responds substantially to a change in price
Demand is *inelastic* if the quantity demanded barely changes from a change in price

Goods with close substitutes have more elastic demand. Like butter and margarine.
If the price of butter falls, less margarine will be purchased.
Contrasted between eggs and butter. If the price of butter falls, the amont of eggs sold won't really change so the demand for eggs is less elastic.

Things we need have a much more inelastic demand. Demand for luxuries is very elastic however. What determines what is a necessity vs a demand is dependant on the person though

A narrow defined market like "margaritas" will have more elastic demand than a broadly defined market like "drinks" which will have a relatively inelastic demand

Goods have more elastic demand has time goes on. If the price of gas rises, at first the demand will stay fairly constant and be inelastic, however as time goes on more people will get bikes or carpool, thereby making the demand more elastic.

To calculate the price elasticy of demand the following formula is used

Price Elasticity of Demand = % change in quantity demanded / % change in price

For example, a 10 percent increase in the price of ice cream causes the amount of ice cream purchased to fall by 20% so:

PED=20/10=2

The elasticity of demand is 2, the change in the amount demanded is twice as much as the change in the price.

The larger the number, the larger the response to a change in price.
