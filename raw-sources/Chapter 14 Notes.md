Firms in Competitive Markets

What is a competitive market
- A market with lots of buyers and sellers
- Trading identical products
	- Because of the first two: each buyer and seller takes the prices given - no individual buyer/seller has enough sway to change the market price alone
- Firms can freely enter and exit the market

Revenue of a competitive firm
- total revenue, TR=PxQ
- Average revenue, AR=TR/Q
- Marginal revenue, MR=Delta TR/ Delta Q
	- Change in TR from an additional unit sold
- For competitive firms
	- AR=P
	- MR=P

MR=P for a competitive firm
A competitive firm
- can keep increasing its output without affectin market price
- So each one unit increase in Q causes revenue to rise by P, MR=P
	- MR=P is only true for firms in competitive markets

Profit Maximization
What Q maximizes a firms profit
- Think at the margin
- If Q increases by 1 unit
	- revenue rises by MR, cost rises by MC
- Compare marginal revenue with marginal cost
	- if MR>MC: increase Q to raise proft
	- if MR<MC: decrease Q to raise profit
	- Maximize profit for Q where MR=MC


Shutdown Vs Exit
Shutdown
- Short run decision not to produce anythin because of market conditions
Exit
- Long run decision to leave the market
Key Difference
- If shutdown in the short run, must still pay fixed costs
- If exiting in the long run, zero costs at all

Short run decision to shutdown
Should a firm shutdown in the short run?
- cost of shutting down = revenue loss
	- =TR
- Benefit of shutting down = cost savings
	- =VC
	- (because the firm will stay pay FC)
- Shutdown if TR < VC, or P < AVC

A firms short run supply curve is the portion of its MC curve above AVC

The Irrelevance of Sunk Costs
Sunk cost
- A cost that has already been committed and cannot be recovered
- should be ignored when making decisions
- you must pay them regardess of your choice
- in the short run FC are sunk costs
	- so FC should not matter in the decision to shut down

A firms long run decision
Should a firm exit or enter in the long run?
- cost of exiting market = revenue loss = TR
- benefit of exiting = cost savings = TC
Firms long run decision
- exit if TR < TC: same as P < ATC
- Enter if TR > TC or P > ATC
The firms LR supply curve is the portion of its MC curve above the LRATC


Market Supply : Assumptions
1. All existing firms and potential entrants have identical costs
2. Each firms cost doent change as other firms enter or exit
3. the number of firms in the market
	1. fixed in the short run because of fixed costs
	2. Variable in the long run due to free entry and exit

The SR Market supply curve
As long as P greater or equal to AVC
- each firm will produce its profit maximizing quantity, where MC=MR
recall from chapter 4:
- At each price, the market quantity supplied is the sum of quantities supplied by all firms

Entry and exit in the long run
- In the long run the number of firms can change due to entry and exit
	- if existing firms earn positive economic profit
		- New firms enter, SR Market supply shifts right
		- P falls, reducing profits and slowing entry
	- If exisitng firms incur losses
		- Some firms exit, SR market supply shifts left
		- P rises, reducing remaining firms losses

The zero profit condition
- Long run equilibrium
	- The process of entry or exit is complete
	- remaining firms earn zero economic profit
- Zero economic profit : when P = ATC
	- since firms produce where P = MR = MC
	- the zero profit condition is P = MC = ATC
	- recall that MC intersects ATC at min ATC
	- Hence in the long run, P = Min ATC
- Why do competitive firms stay in business if they make zero profit?
	- profit = total revenue - total cost
	- totcal cost includes all implicit costs like the opportunity cost of the owners time and money
	- Zero-profit equilibrium
		- economic profit is 0
		- accounting profit is positive

Long run supply curve
- Long run supply curve is horizontal if
	- all firms have identical costs
	- costs do not change as firms enter or exit the market
- Long run Suply curve might slope upward if
	- firms have different costs
	- or costs rise as firms enter the market
- Firms have different costs
	- as P rises, firms with lower costs enter the market before those with higher costs
	- Further increases in P make it worthwhile for higher-cost firms to enter the market, which increases market quantity supplied
	- Hence, LR market supply curve slopes upward

Efficiancy of a competitive market
- profit maximization Q ehere MC=MR
	- perfect competition P = MR
	- So, in the competitive equilibrium, P = MC
- the competitive eq is efficient
	- maximizes total surplus because P = MC
		- MC is the cost of producing the marginal unit
		- P is value to buyers of the marginal unit
